## Context: How Morningstar Scores These Stocks

Morningstar's star rating is driven by four factors: **moat strength** (how durable the competitive advantage is), **fair value estimate** (DCF-based), **uncertainty** (how confident they are in the FMV), and **current price**. A 5-star stock means the current price represents an "excessively pessimistic" scenario — 75% probability the true value is above today's price.

**Critically: not all FMVs are equal.** Analyst-driven FMVs (backed by a named analyst, live DCF, and explicit reconfirmation) are far more credible than quantitative model estimates. This distinction matters a lot for how you should weight the screener signals.

## One More Thing: Read the Moat Before the Discount

Morningstar's system is designed so that the **moat determines how much discount you need** before it's worth buying:
- **Medium uncertainty** (IQV, MSFT): need 30% discount for 5 stars → both qualify
- **High uncertainty**: need 40% discount → changes the math significantly for ZTS (if they were to issue a High uncertainty rating)

This is why ZTS's situation is dangerous: if the uncertainty were reclassified to High (which competition + guidance cut might warrant), the 5-star threshold moves from 30% off FMV to 40% off — and the current "discount" might not even clear that bar.
